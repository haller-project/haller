# Model card: Haller-1

Haller-1 is the first release of Haller, the handwritten BIP39 word reader of
the handwritten seed reader of a SeedSigner fork. It is made of four small
networks, run offline on the device in 16-bit integer arithmetic by
SeedSigner's C library.

## Components

| File | Role | Architecture | Parameters | Input | SHA-256 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `haller-1-capitals.npz` | word reader, capitals expert: reads every photo | 4 convolutional blocks (16/32/64/64 channels) and 2 dilated 1-D convolutions (128), CTC over 37 classes (blank, a-z case-insensitive, 0-9) | 163,557 | word strip, 32 px high, at most 256 px wide | `2e9275f1acd0d347437e856160430e96a283ec484a48ab96a2ea1ca91531fe14` |
| `haller-1-lowercase.npz` | word reader, lowercase and cursive expert: second reading of the photos whose seed is not valid, alone and blended with the capitals expert | same as above | 163,557 | same | `5d340b354ced032992cb474f03bcc0b063f24ee8e1446d2953203b778652e509` |
| `haller-1-detector.npz` | word detector: words, line numbers, card area, card corners and kernels, number-word links and partial words, on every photo | U-Net of 3x3 convolutions (8-16-32-48-64-64-64-64, decoder 32-32-16-8), 11 output maps at half resolution | 223,531 | photo reduced to 288 x 384, grayscale | `8ddddf19ee0088e8dcfe71ba12377167feeb6a626cd6edb17b0bed3b5f69fc5b` |
| `haller-1-fingerprint.npz` | fingerprint reader: reads the line holding the master key fingerprint (8 hexadecimal digits) written under the words; a seed is verified only when this fingerprint matches the one of the words | same as the word readers, fine-tuned from the capitals expert | 163,557 | line strip, as the word readers | `b92cca552dc0a718d309a98b3e5f7ed6f20ee0be32f92aec8df1804d809de454` |

Parameters are the integer weights and biases of the files (batch
normalization folded). All four use 16-bit weights and 12-bit activations;
the word readers output log-probabilities every 4 input columns, scored
against the 2,048 English BIP39 words by a dictionary decoder; the detector
costs about 300 million multiply-accumulates per photo.

**Engine around the networks** (SeedSigner, not part of this release): page
detection and word cropping (`page.py`), normalization of the ink
(`preprocess.py`), decoding against the BIP39 list, temperature 2.2 on the
word scores, BIP39 checksum analysis over the 512 most likely combinations,
and the confidence rules that decide whether a seed is reported as valid or
which words are marked uncertain. The numbers below measure the engine with
these weights; a change in the engine changes them.

## Intended use

- Reading a BIP39 seed of 12 to 24 English words written by hand, on an
  official SeedSigner seed card or on a free list (blank or ruled paper, one
  or two columns, numbered or not), from a photo taken by the device camera.
- On an offline signing device: photos stay in memory, nothing is sent.
- **The user reviews every word against the paper.** A seed reported as
  valid can still be wrong (a wrong reading that passes the checksum); the
  reader is an input aid, not a verifier.

Out of scope: languages other than English BIP39; non-BIP39 secrets;
screenshots or printed text; reading without the review step; any use where a
wrong word could move funds without a human check.

Definitions (per photo): **exact**, every word displayed is the written one;
**valid and exact**, exact and reported valid with no word marked;

wrong). Intervals are 95 % Wilson intervals over photos, and for words a
bootstrap over writers and photos.

### Calibration

The word scores divided by a temperature are used as log-likelihoods: the
probability of each word given the checksum is the percentage shown next to a
proposed word, and a word below 0.95 is marked uncertain. The temperature
(2.2) and the thresholds were chosen on 640 simulated pages kept apart from
every test set, as the lowest temperature with no wrong seed reported valid.

## Known limitations

- **A valid checksum does not prove a correct reading.** One random wrong
  combination of words in 16 passes the checksum of a 12-word seed (1 in 256
  for 24 words). The engine reports a seed as valid only when its own
  probability is high, but adversarial or unlucky pages can still produce a
  false valid seed. The review against the paper is required.
- **Faint letters.** The normalization keeps ink above a share of the darkest
  stroke; letters written with a skipping pen or very light pressure at the
  start or end of a word can vanish, and the rest of the word can be read with
  confidence as another BIP39 word (for example ADDRESS read as DRESS). Write
  with dark, even ink.
- **Truncated words.** A word cut by the frame of the photo, by a large gap
  inside the word, or by a crop that misses its ends is the main source of
  confident wrong readings.
- **Fast cursive** is read worst; lowercase and cursive less well than
  capitals; uncertain words are marked.
- **Two-column lists.** The order (column by column or row by row) is decided
  by the line numbers when they are written; when both orders give a
  plausible seed, every word is marked uncertain. Columns written very close
  together can be read as one.
- **Layouts with more than two columns, several documents in the photo, and
  seed card templates other than SeedSigner's** are not supported; ruled
  notebook paper can be taken for a seed card.
- **Look-alike words** (shop and ship, review and renew, item and ten): a
  confident wrong reading is possible when the writing is ambiguous.
- **Crossed-out words and corrections** can be read instead of the
  correction.
- **Blurry photos, very small writing**: the page is not found or the words
  are not read; the user is asked to take the photo again.
- **Evaluation coverage.** Real photos are few compared with simulated ones,
  and come from a small number of writers; see the counts above.

## Ethical and security considerations

A seed phrase gives full control of the funds it protects. The reader is meant
for an offline device that keeps photos in memory only. The weights contain
no seed and were trained on no real seed. Test seeds used for evaluation are
random, valid and never used for funds; the photos of the test set are not
published while the set is in use.

## Reproducibility

The recipe rebuilds the published weights from public inputs:
[`recipes/haller-1/README.md`](recipes/haller-1/README.md). Quantization and
the reference vectors are reproduced byte for byte from the published float
models; training on a GPU gives models of the same level, not the same bits.
The simulated evaluation strips, derived from EMNIST, are not stored: they
are regenerated identically from the official EMNIST archive
(`recipes/haller-1/evalsim/regenerate.sh`).
The detector recipe, [`recipes/haller-1-detector/README.md`](recipes/haller-1-detector/README.md),
gives the frozen code of its 137 training and validation shards (made before
the variety audit of the later synthetic data), their content digests and
the manifest of the photos they were composed on, and gives the shipped
detector byte for byte from its published float model and activation ranges.

## Versioning

`haller-N` is a new training of the weights; `haller-N.M` is a change without
retraining (calibration, thresholds, corrected quantization). Each version is
a git tag of this repository and a release that carries the weights and their
SHA-256 digests; see [`RELEASING.md`](RELEASING.md) and
[`CHANGELOG.md`](CHANGELOG.md).
